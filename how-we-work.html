How we work

Fees agreed up front, and no stake in whether you transact.

This page covers what we do, the order it happens in and how long each stage takes. We agree each fee before the work begins. Each service stands on its own, and none obliges you to the next.

Our services

For every engagement, we agree the fee before any work begins and quote it after a first conversation. The agreement audit, buy-in review and readiness diagnostic carry a fixed fee, preparation is a retainer and transaction support is hourly. There is no success fee on any engagement. The next section shows the order the work happens in and how long each stage takes.

Agreement audit

Two weeks. We read your buy-sell, operating agreement and any equity documents, then give you a findings list. It covers how price is set, whether discounts apply, what is funded and for which triggers, who can veto a buyer, and what happens on death, disability or divorce.

Buy-in review, for the next generation

Two weeks, for an advisor who has been offered equity. We cover what it is, what it is worth, whether it works against your income and what should be in the document. The review is confidential to you.

Readiness diagnostic

Six weeks. We deliver the full picture to the partner group: structure, client transferability, restated economics, a scorecard against what a buyer's diligence team will test and a sequenced plan. It includes interviews with the next generation conducted without the founder present.

Preparation retainer

We do the work rather than only describe it, on a retainer. That means relationship transition waves, restating the books, rewriting the agreement, documenting the service model, building the bench and supporting the conversations between founder and next-generation advisor that neither can easily have alone.

Transaction support

We work at an hourly rate, alongside your banker and counsel, on your side of the table. The work covers diligence preparation, reviewing structures and earnout mechanics (the part of the price paid later, depending on results) and modeling what each offer nets you.

Firm and team workshops

A half-day or full-day session for a broker-dealer, custodian, study group or advisor cohort. We run the diagnostic live with the room's own numbers and add the succession conversation framework for founders and next-generation advisors.

The order it happens in

Something good is never easy Preparing a practice to pass on is deliberate work done in sequence, and it starts with understanding where the practice stands today.

Steps 1 and 2 come first whatever the findings, because the later steps depend on them and relationship transition takes the longest.

1

Find out what is true

We review the agreement, restate the economics and check who owns each client relationship according to the CRM rather than anyone's memory. The gap between the CRM and memory is often the finding.

Weeks 1 to 6
2

Start moving relationships early

This starts before the other steps are finished, because it is the only item measured in years. It uses structured joint meetings with the largest households, with someone other than the founder leading the agenda.

Month 2 onward, ongoing
3

Make the practice easy to evaluate

We move the books to accrual accounting, document the add-backs (personal and one-time costs a buyer adds back to profit), write a service model the CRM can show was delivered, and rewrite the agreement to match what the firm has become.

Months 3 to 18
4

Decide the path, then run it

The options are an internal sale, an outside sale, a platform, a phased step-back or continuing to practice. Sale paths are compared on what each nets after tax rather than on headline price. If you sell, diligence preparation follows, alongside your banker and counsel.

Month 18 onward

What we do not do

We do not appraise practices for regulatory or tax purposes, broker transactions, sell securities, manage money or accept compensation from buyers, lenders or platforms. When you need a formal valuation, an intermediary, counsel or a tax advisor, we will tell you and help you choose one.

Why there is no success fee Many firms in this field are paid when a transaction closes, which gives them a financial interest in a sale. We charge a retainer for preparation and an hourly rate for transaction support, with no success fee and no referral fee from a buyer, lender or platform. Recommending that you wait does not change what we earn.
