How Carry Forward works

Something good is never easy Building a practice takes years, and preparing it to pass on takes the same sustained effort. That work starts with understanding where the practice stands today.
Many firms in this field are paid when a transaction closes. We are not. We charge a retainer for preparation and an hourly rate for transaction support, with no success fee and no referral fee from a buyer, lender or platform. Recommending that you wait does not change what we earn.

Advisors inside wirehouses, insurance career systems and broker-dealers can face a different question, because the book may not be freely salable. We work with advisors in that situation. The order of the work, with timing, is on the How we work page.

Two different conversations

A succession involves two advisors with different questions: the one who built the practice and the one expected to carry it forward. We work with each of them, usually one at a time before bringing them together.

If you built the practice

If you expect to sell, transfer or step back from the practice within the next 2 to 10 years, the plan you build now shapes the choices available to you later.

  • What your practice is worth today, and what is driving that number
  • What the same practice could be worth once it is prepared to pass on
  • Whether a buyer, inside your firm or outside it, could finance the price you have in mind
  • What your buy-sell agreement, if you have one, already commits you to
  • What would happen to your clients and your family if you were unable to work before a plan is in place

If you are the next generation

If you have been offered equity, it helps to know which kind it is, what it is worth, whether you can afford it and what your staying is worth to the person offering it.
