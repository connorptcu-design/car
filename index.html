The gap nobody is paid to fill

Valuation is close to free. Custodians provide it, and specialist firms will produce one for a few thousand dollars. Deal execution is well served too, by experienced bankers who run competitive processes and earn a fee when the transaction closes.

What has no owner is the twenty-four to thirty-six months in between. Moving relationships off one person. Converting a group of advisors sharing an office into a firm with one set of books. Restating financials so they survive a quality-of-earnings review. Writing the agreement that should have been written a decade ago. Building the next generation who will either buy the firm or be the reason someone else wants to.

That work is where nearly all of the value sits, and almost nobody sells it, for a structural reason: everyone else in the room is paid when a transaction closes. Which means nobody else in the room can tell you to wait three years.

We take no success fee, on any engagement, ever. We are paid a retainer for preparation work and an hourly rate if you want us alongside you in a transaction. We never take a percentage of a deal, we never receive compensation from a buyer, lender or platform, and we do not sell securities. It means the advice to wait is always available to us, and it is the right advice more often than the industry lets on.

Two very different conversations

Founders and the generation behind them are worried about opposite things, and they rarely say so to each other. We work with both, usually separately before ever together.

You built the practice

You are somewhere between five and fifteen years from being done, and the decision has no deadline, so it keeps not happening.

  • What it is worth today, and why
  • What it would be worth prepared, on the same profit
  • Whether an internal buyer could actually finance it
  • What your agreement already decided for you
  • What happens to your family if this week goes badly
