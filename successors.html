You were promised equity. Nobody has told you what that means.

Four completely different things get called equity in advisory firms. Two of them leave you owning something. Before you negotiate the percentage, find out which one is on the table.

Work out the buy-in Book a review

Ask which of these it is, in these words

Real equity. Shares or units you buy and then own.

The only version you can sell. It is also the only one you have to fund, which is why firms often offer one of the others and use the same word.

Profits interest. A share of everything the firm becomes worth above today's value.

Genuinely good and badly underused. No cheque up front, because the threshold is set at today's value, so you are only paid on what you help create. It lives in an LLC and needs an 83(b) election filed within thirty days. Miss that window and the whole thing becomes ordinary income.

Phantom equity or appreciation rights. A future cash payment that tracks what equity would have been worth.

A bonus plan with a spreadsheet attached. It can pay real money, but you own nothing, you cannot sell it, it usually dies if you leave, and the firm can often amend the plan.

Revenue or profit share. A percentage of what the firm brings in.

Not equity in any sense. Reasonable as compensation, unreasonable as an answer to the ownership question.

What your staying is worth to them

A buyer prices a firm on whether the clients stay once the founder leaves. If you are the reason they would, the firm is worth meaningfully more with you in it than without you. For a mid-sized practice that gap often runs to seven figures.

Nobody has told you that number, and it is your entire negotiating position. Do not use it as a threat. Use it as the shared problem it actually is: the firm is worth more if the clients know you, and neither of you gets there without a written agreement and three years of joint meetings. You are asking to be pointed at the thing that makes you both money.

Hire your own lawyer The firm's counsel represents the firm, which in practice means the founder. They may be a good person who likes you. They are still not your lawyer, and the few thousand dollars is the best-spent money in the entire transaction.
